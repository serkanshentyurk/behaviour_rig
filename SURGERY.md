# Head-plate surgery (Zapit)

The surgery for the Zapit mice: a head-plate, with bregma and a 3 mm reference point marked on the skull so Zapit can be aligned to the brain. Steps marked **(Zapit only)** can be skipped for a mouse that only needs a head-plate. Taken from the lab's protocol document, which also covers fibre implantation; that part isn't included here.

Handling and water restriction afterwards are in [TRAINING.md](TRAINING.md#from-surgery-to-the-first-session).

## The day before

1. Give the mouse unmedicated jelly.

## Materials

**Non-aseptic**
- micro applicators (purple and blue)
- air blower, Vaseline, eye cream, tape
- Hibiscrub, cotton swabs
- dental cement (monomer, catalyst, polymer), and ice
- Green Activator (10% citric acid solution), tiny sponges
- UV glue, curing light
- head-bar holder, spirit level

**Aseptic**
- autoclaved surgery tools, drill bits and head-plates
- an autoclaved box of consumables (cotton buds, triangle sponge points, micro applicators etc.)
- autoclaved aluminium foil and paper towels
- drape
- 21G (green) and 23G (blue) needles
- Hibiscrub
- insulin syringe with saline

## Preparing the surgery table

1. **Heat pad.** Tape a Kimwipe around it and switch it on. Keep pressing the button through START, MOUSE, SET POINT: 37.2 and SET TIME, and leave it flickering at SET TIME; that keeps the pad heated indefinitely.
2. **Isoflurane.** Check the level is between the two lines. If not, top it up; the bottles are in the drug box and the surgery cupboards.
3. **Scavenger.** A new one weighs 1200 g and can be used up to 1400 g. Weigh it and write the date and weight on the container. Above 1400 g, dispose of it and get a new one.
4. **Ear bars.** Find a matching pair. Ones with pointy ends are more accurate, but more dangerous.

## Preparing the mouse

1. If the mouse is group-housed, it stays in its original cage and its cage mates move to a new one: transfer the cage labels, enrichment, food, water bottle and some of the bedding to a new cage, move the cage mates into it, and leave it in the holding room.
2. Add a flat grid, a cardboard house, a wet mash, food pellets and a water bottle to the surgery mouse's cage.
3. Put a surgery card and a flat-grid label on the cage.
4. Bring the cage to the surgery room and put it on the heating rack, without switching the heat on.

## Preparing the induction room

1. Weigh the mouse and record the weight on the surgery card.
2. Work out its Metacam volume: 5 mg/kg, which is the weight in grams × 2.5 µl (50 µl for a 20 g mouse).
3. Draw it up in a 0.5 or 1 ml insulin syringe, with no bubbles, and put it next to the induction box.
4. Find a working hair trimmer and clean off any hair left on it.
5. Have some white tape ready, for removing small pieces of hair.

**Throughout the surgery:**
- Keep the body temperature at 36.5–37 °C; around 36 °C is common.
- Keep the breathing and the depth of anaesthesia right by adjusting the isoflurane. Gasping or jumpy breathing means the concentration is too high; fast, shallow breaths mean it is too low and the mouse is getting closer to waking.
- A typical isoflurane course: induction 4%, shaving 3%, start of surgery 3%, 2% once the ear bars are in, 1.25–1.75% for the rest of the surgery (smaller mice may need more), then reduce gradually from 1.25% near the end.
- Switch the scavenger, oxygen and isoflurane on in that order, and off in the reverse order.

## Induction

1. Put a clean paper towel in the induction box, put the mouse in and close the lid.
2. Turn on the scavenger, oxygen (level 1) and isoflurane (4%). Check that the isoflurane is in box mode.
3. Switch on the cage heat rack.
4. When the mouse breathes at about 1 Hz (one breath per second), lower the isoflurane to 3%, switch to mask mode and move the mouse to the mask.
5. **Metacam, under the skin.** With the mouse lying flat, pull the loose skin on its back upwards, insert the needle horizontally, inject, and pull the syringe out horizontally. Dispose of it in the sharps bin.

## Shaving the head

1. Hold the mouse in your non-dominant hand, gently pulling the skin of the head backwards with your thumb. Hold the trimmer in your other hand like a pen.
2. Shave from the back of the head towards the front, in small movements so you don't damage the whiskers or eyes. Remove all hair from between the ears to just behind the eyes.
3. Watch the breathing as you shave. Put the mouse back on the mask before it regains consciousness, and wait for 1 Hz breathing before carrying on.
4. When you have finished, let the breathing settle at 1 Hz on the mask. Then switch off the isoflurane, oxygen and scavenger, and quickly take the mouse to the surgery table.

## Non-aseptic preparation

1. Turn on the scavenger, oxygen (level 1) and isoflurane (3%).
2. Roll the tongue out with the hard end of a micro applicator, and hook the front teeth onto the bite bar. Pull the body gently backwards with both hands to check the teeth are secure.
3. Arrange the body and paws with the micro applicator so the mouse lies in a natural, symmetrical position. This helps the oxygen flow and stops the body tipping over.
4. Apply eye cream.
5. **Ear bars.**
   - Put them on the ear-bar holders with the thin ends towards the head, and tighten them so they move sideways but not up or down.
   - Move both bars together until the tips are about 1 mm in front of and above where the ear canals start. Adjust the A/P and D/V coordinates to find the spot. If the mouse is too small for the ear bars to reach, put a folded paper towel under the heat pad.
   - Push the bars gently into the head. The head should bulge out only slightly, and you should feel some resistance. If the eyes or head bulge a lot, or the head feels soft, the bars are in the wrong place.
   - Hold them in place and fix them one at a time. Push the head down gently with a micro applicator to check it is secure.
   - Bring the isoflurane mask closer to the nose.
6. Put the nose bar on the nose and tighten it just enough to hold it; the bones of the nose are delicate.
7. Insert the rectal temperature probe, a third of its length, with Vaseline. Keep it straight, tape it to the heat pad, and tape the cable to the table so it can't move.
8. With 4–5 cotton swabs, apply plenty of Hibiscrub to the shaved area and the hair around it.

## Aseptic phase

### Preparation

1. Open the sterile gloves and surgical gown. Rub Sterillium on your hands for at least 30 s, then put on the gown, then the gloves.
2. Wrap everything you will touch during the aseptic phase in autoclaved aluminium foil.
3. Apply aseptic Hibiscrub to the head, swabbing from the centre outwards.
4. Cut the drape in half. Lay one half over the body, leaving only the head exposed, and tape it in place. Use the other half as an extra aseptic surface.

### Incision

1. Pinch a toe to check for a pain reflex. If there is one, increase the isoflurane and check again after 1 min.
2. Under the microscope, grasp the skin at the centre of the head with forceps and cut it with scissors.
3. Let the skin retract, check the exposed area, and make more cuts as needed. The opening has to include your target area, with room around it for cement, and where the head-plate will go. One large, clean incision is better than several small ones, though hard to achieve. If the incision exposes neck muscles at the back, these can be removed.
4. Remove the periosteum (the transparent, slimy layer between the skin and the skull) with forceps and scissors. This is essential for the cement to stick.

### Etching the skull

1. With fine forceps, scratch all the exposed skull except the sutures: thoroughly, but not so much that it weakens the skull, which makes the head-plate more likely to come off during training.
2. If scratching isn't enough, etch it chemically with the Green Activator. Put a drop on an aseptic surface (not the skull), dip a piece of sponge held in forceps, and apply it to the skull without touching the skin.
3. Wash the Activator off immediately with saline. Hold a triangle sponge point in forceps in your non-dominant hand and the saline syringe in the other; add a drop, soak it up, and repeat until the whole surface is clean.

### Marking bregma and lambda

1. Mark the sutures, bregma and lambda with a fine pen. To redraw, wet the area with saline and rub gently with a triangle sponge point.

### Levelling the head

1. Set the head to −3 mm D/V as a starting point.
2. Attach a needle holder to the stereotaxic frame, with a 21G (green) needle; a 23G (blue) one is too short. Rotate the needle until the bevel is visible.
3. Move the needle to bregma. With the microscope at 45°, zoomed in close, bring the tip down until it just touches bregma, and zero D/V on the coordinate display.
4. Lift the needle, move it straight to lambda, and read D/V. The head is level when bregma and lambda differ by no more than 0.05 mm either way.
5. If it isn't level, adjust the head slightly and repeat.

### (Zapit only) Marking the 3 mm reference point

1. Move the needle to bregma and zero A/P.
2. Move the needle 3 mm anterior, on the midline. Lower it until it lightly touches the skull and leaves a visible groove, then move it out of the way. Mark the groove with a fine pen.
3. With the microscope at 90°, zoom in on the mark. Holding the drill like a pen and steadying it with your other hand, drill a shallow, rounded groove at the mark. Press lightly, so you don't go through the skull.
4. Mark the groove with a pen. If the point tends to have faded by the time the Zapit sessions start, draw a large cross on it, or a row of points (for example at 1, 2 and 3 mm), so it is easier to find.

### Head-plate

1. Attach a head-plate holder to the stereotaxic frame and tighten it with an Allen key. Put an autoclaved head-plate in it.
2. Level the head-plate with a spirit level, in both pitch and roll.
3. Move it into position, then raise it out of the way.
   - **(Zapit only)** If the mouse will be used for Zapit scanning across the whole dorsal surface, place the head-plate at the far back, so its centre hole is over lambda.
4. Remove condensation from the cement tray and mix dental cement with a purple micro applicator: 4 drops of monomer, 1 drop of catalyst, 1 small scoop of polymer.
   - **(Zapit only)** Use the transparent polymer (pink container), so the skull and the marks stay visible. Otherwise use the opaque one (green container).
5. Cover all the exposed skull with a thin layer of cement.
   - **(Zapit only)** Don't smudge the sutures, bregma or the reference point.
6. Put another blob of cement at the back, where the head-plate will sit, and lower the head-plate until it just touches the head.
7. Fill the three holes in the head-plate with cement, then apply a generous amount over the top and back of the plate. A P20 pipette is very useful for filling the gaps between the plate and the skull.
8. Wait until the cement is completely dry: tapped gently with a surgical tool, it should feel solid. Clean the cement tray before the rest sets.
9. Optional: apply a thin layer of super glue around the edges of the cement.

This is the end of the aseptic phase.

## UV glue tower

1. Apply a small amount of UV glue in a circle at the centre of the head-plate, and cure it with the UV light for 5 s. Don't look directly at the light.
2. Repeat 2–3 times to build up a tower, then cure it for another 20 s.

## End of surgery

1. Lower the isoflurane to 1%.
2. Remove the drape, the head-plate holder, the temperature probe, the ear bars and the nose bar.
3. Switch off the isoflurane, oxygen and scavenger.
4. Weigh the mouse, put it in its cage, and leave the cage on the heat rack.
5. Clean up: collect your tools, move the microscope out of the way, switch off the heat pad, put the drill bit in the sharps bin, and wipe the surgery table with 70% ethanol.
6. Once the mouse has fully recovered (moving, eating or drinking, not falling over), put a wet mash and meloxicam jelly in the cage. If it hasn't recovered after 45 min, watch it closely and ask the NRF for advice.
7. Take the cage back to the holding room, and record the procedure and the weight in PyRAT.

## After surgery

1. 24 hours after surgery, put another wet mash and meloxicam jelly in the cage.
2. For 2 days after surgery, watch the mouse's recovery closely and record its weight in PyRAT.
3. If the wound bleeds or forms scabs, apply Vetericyn with a cotton swab several times a day. It doesn't always work.
4. Handling starts the day after surgery, and water restriction no earlier than 4 days after it; see [TRAINING.md](TRAINING.md#from-surgery-to-the-first-session).
