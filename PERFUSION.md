# Perfusion

The lab's perfusion protocol, for experiments that need the brains. It is included here as the lab wrote it, and it hasn't been used with this set-up yet.

> [!WARNING]
> **The Dolethal dose is missing.** The protocol says to draw up twice the volume the mouse's weight needs, but not what that volume is. Learn perfusion from someone who does it, and get the dose from them, before your first one.

Do everything in a perfusion room, and every step that involves PFA inside a fume hood.

## Materials (per mouse)

- 16% PFA, in a 10 ml brown glass vial. Ask Stores for "16% Formaldehyde", or dilute the concentrated PFA in the lab fridge to 16%.
- 1X PBS, made up from the concentrated PBS in the lab fridge.
- Two 50 ml tubes.
- 70% Dolethal: 0.7 ml Dolethal and 0.3 ml saline.
- If the mouse is group-housed, an empty cage with old bedding and a hiding place.
- PPE: goggles, white lab coat, mask, gloves, hair cover.

## Setting up

1. Check that the waste container under the fume hood is not full.
2. Set the perfusion pump to ×1 speed and turn the dial to the 7 o'clock position.
3. Connect a butterfly needle to the pump, put it in the waste container, and run the pump for 1 min to clear any liquid left in the system.
4. Put a surgical platform with a rubber mat in the middle of the fume hood. Put spring-loaded fine scissors and tweezers on its left, and surgery scissors and a needle holder on its right.
5. Stick five 23G (blue) needles into the rubber mat.

## PFA and PBS

1. **PFA tube.** Break the top off the PFA vial, pour it into a new 50 ml tube, add 30 ml of 1X PBS to make 4% PFA, and label it.
2. **PBS tube.** Pour 50 ml of 1X PBS into a new 50 ml tube and label it.
3. Put both tubes in a rack.

## Preparing the pump

1. Put both the PFA and the PBS line in the PBS tube.
2. Set the pump valve to the PFA line and run the pump; this washes out any PFA left in the system.
3. Once PBS reaches the butterfly needle, turn the valve to the PBS line and keep the pump running for 30 s, or until there are no air bubbles left.
4. Switch off the pump and move the PFA line to the PFA tube. Check that both lines reach the bottom of their tubes.

## Dolethal injection

It is a good idea to learn the scruffing technique and the injection on a mouse being killed by injection under Schedule 1 before your first perfusion.

1. Weigh the mouse.
2. With a 1 ml syringe, draw up twice the volume of Dolethal its weight needs. **The dose is missing from this protocol** (see the warning at the top).
3. Fit a 27G (grey) needle and expel the excess until the syringe holds the right volume, with no air bubbles.
4. Scruff the mouse and inject the Dolethal intraperitoneally.
5. Put the mouse back in its cage.

## Exposing the heart

1. When the mouse breathes at about 1 Hz and has lost its righting reflex, move it to the surgical platform.
2. Pinch a toe hard to check it is unconscious.
3. Pin each paw into the rubber mat with a 23G needle.
4. With tweezers and surgery scissors, make a small horizontal cut through the skin just below the rib cage, leaving the organs intact.
5. Extend the cut upwards into a U shape until the diaphragm is visible, without damaging the heart.
6. Cut the diaphragm to expose the heart, and continue the U-shaped cut until the heart is fully visible.
7. Pin the flap of the abdominal wall into the mat with a 23G needle.

## Inserting the needle

1. Check that you can see the right atrium (darker, small) and the left ventricle (lighter, large, slightly towards the back).
2. With tweezers in your left hand, hold the heart at the right ventricle. With your right hand, insert the butterfly needle into the most prominent point of the left ventricle.
   - Keep the needle in your right hand until the end of the perfusion, when you switch off the pump. Keep it as still as you can so you don't puncture anything else; if you can't, hold it with a clip.
3. With the spring-loaded scissors in your left hand, snip the right atrium.

## Perfusion

1. Check that the pump valve is set to the PBS line, then start the pump. Dark red blood should start pooling around the body. If the pooling fluid is clear, PBS is leaking and the needle is in the wrong place.
2. When the pooling liquid starts to turn clear, turn the valve to the PFA line.
3. Wait 30–60 s for the perfusion tremor, then 1 min more.
4. Switch off the pump, and remove the butterfly needle and the pins.
5. Check that the mouse is flat and stiff.

## Collecting the brain

1. Cut off the head at the neck with scissors.
2. Either remove the brain now, or drop-fix the whole head and remove the brain the next day.

**Removing the brain now**
1. Hold the head upside down, nose down. Holding it by the head-plate with a needle holder can make this easier.
2. Cut the left and right sides of the vertebra with scissors, and remove any excess skin around the head.
3. Pick around the vertebra with tweezers, removing the flesh bit by bit and cutting any large pieces of bone with scissors. Work forwards along the sides towards the nose.
4. The olfactory bulb is delicate and hard to remove, but useful for mounting when imaging.
5. Put the brain in the PFA tube and leave it in the fridge for 24–48 hours. Then replace the PFA with 1X PBS. For long-term storage, add sodium azide to stop bacterial growth.

**Drop-fixing**

Drop-fixing makes the brain firmer and less likely to be damaged, but it can also make it brittle, and may reduce antibody access for later steps such as immunohistochemistry.
1. Put the whole head in the PFA tube and store it in the fridge.
2. The next day, remove the brain as above. Because it is already fixed, it can go straight into 1X PBS in the fridge, with sodium azide for long-term storage.

## Cleaning up

1. Put the remaining body parts in a red bag.
2. Pour any liquid on the surgical platform into the waste container.
3. Rinse the platform with water inside the fume hood and pour that into the waste container too. Repeat.
4. Rinse and scrub the platform and the tools in the sink outside.
5. Run the pump with the butterfly needle in the waste container until the system is empty.
6. Put the butterfly needle in the sharps bin.
7. Wipe down the fume hood with disinfectant.
